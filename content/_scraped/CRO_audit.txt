Reflexivity website: CRO and narrative
audit
The assessment
The new site has a stronger and more differentiated foundation than the existing one. It moves
beyond a generic “faster AI research” pitch and begins to articulate what makes Reflexivity
distinctive: the Knowledge Graph, proactive intelligence, and analysis an investor can inspect.

The biggest opportunity is to make those pieces feel like one coherent investment workflow,
rather than a collection of impressive capabilities. A visitor should quickly understand:

   1.​ What Reflexivity is and who it is for.
   2.​ What it does when an investor has a question.
   3.​ What it does when they are not actively asking.
   4.​ Why its relationship intelligence changes the answer.
   5.​ How the investor can verify the work.
   6.​ What they will see when they request a demo.

The most urgent conversion issue is the demo destination: I could not find an accessible
booking form, scheduler, or contact action on the prototype’s demo page during this
review. The page explains the walkthrough, but I could not complete or fully test a demo
request. This may be unfinished prototype work or a rendering issue, so it should be verified in
the intended production environment before launch.

How to read this audit

   ●​ Verified issue means I observed the specific copy or behavior on the prototype.
   ●​ Recommendation means a proposed improvement, not a claim that the current site is
      broken.
   ●​ Confirm before publishing flags claims that depend on product, legal, security, data, or
      client approval.
   ●​ I reviewed the core homepage, demo, product, use-case, deployment, customer,
      security, and company templates—not every page or every device state.
   ●​ I compared the prototype with the existing Reflexivity site and the attached messaging,
      capabilities, moat, and Knowledge Graph materials. I have not independently verified
      internal product claims or customer permissions.

Positioning and language
    ●​ Name the category in the hero: “AI investment research platform for institutional
       investors,” rather than relying on the smaller “AI Investment Research” label alone.
    ●​ Keep Reflexivity and Alfred distinct: Reflexivity is the platform; Alfred is its AI analyst.
    ●​ Introduce the Knowledge Graph as the relationship-intelligence layer, not as a
       separate product visitors must understand before they understand the platform.
    ●​ Explain the two modes together: on-demand analysis when the investor asks, and
       proactive intelligence when relevant developments emerge.
    ●​ Connect capabilities to outcomes: show how relationship intelligence reveals
       exposure, monitoring identifies a development, and Alfred helps investigate the
       implication.
    ●​ Use “coverage universe” only after giving a plain-language example, such as
       companies and themes the visitor follows.
    ●​ Distinguish alerts, Insights, monitoring, and scheduled research so these terms do
       not appear interchangeable.
    ●​ Replace broad promises with a concrete investment task wherever a visitor might
       reasonably ask, “What would that look like for me?”
    ●​ Use a consistent vocabulary for the Knowledge Graph, Insights, Alfred, and
       deployment options across all pages.
    ●​ Avoid positioning the platform as only a chat interface. The monitoring, relationship
       layer, and workflow context are important to the larger story.
    ●​ Avoid overstating what general-purpose AI or competing terminals cannot do.
       Compare specific workflows and evidence requirements rather than making absolute
       competitor claims.
    ●​ Separate current functionality from roadmap concepts. Persistent workflows,
       scheduled reruns, personalized briefs, and thesis monitoring should be published as
       current only after product confirmation.


Suggested core narrative
      Investment research that sees the connections—and shows its work.​
      Reflexivity is an AI investment research platform for institutional investors. Ask
      Alfred to investigate a question or test a thesis, while always-on intelligence
      surfaces developments across the companies and themes you follow. Underneath
      both is a Knowledge Graph that maps market relationships, supported by
      institutional data and analysis you can inspect.

This is a proposed direction, not a claim that every feature and data entitlement works
identically for every customer.

Recommended implementation order
1. Before launch

Finish and test the demo path; remove placeholders; correct misleading labels and links; verify
security, availability, and performance claims; and establish one approved set of metrics.

2. Strengthen the main story

Clarify the homepage proposition, introduce the two modes, explain how the Knowledge Graph
connects them, and show one complete investment workflow with inspectable evidence.

3. Build buyer confidence

Improve the customer proof, audience-specific use cases, data transparency, deployment
comparison, and security procurement path.

4. Optimize with evidence

Instrument the full demo funnel, gather sales feedback, and test messaging and page variations
against qualified meetings—not just traffic or click-through rate.

Bottom line: the site does not need more feature claims. It needs a clearer demonstration of
how Reflexivity helps an investor move from question or market event → connected
evidence → analysis they can inspect → an informed next step.
